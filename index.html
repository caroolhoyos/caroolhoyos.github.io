Master’s thesis

Hardness of the Ad Hoc Design of Electoral Systems for Achieving a Desired Outcome

Grade: 10/10 · Highest honors

My Master’s Thesis focuses on the study of the hardness of designing a multi-round electoral system from a given voter preference profile. In each round, a scoring vector is applied, and the objective is to determine whether there exists a choice of such vectors under which a candidate previously designated by the organizer is elected. Therefore, the electoral system is not assumed to be fixed, but is instead regarded as a part of the decision problem itself.

In the first part, several variants of the problem are formally defined according to the number of rounds allowed and the possible values that the scoring vectors may take. The computational complexity of these variants is then analysed. In particular, the NP-Completeness of some variants is proved by a polynomial-time reduction from 3-SAT. Furthermore, the APX-Hardness of the corresponding optimization version of some variants is established.

In the second part, a genetic algorithm is proposed as a heuristic approach for solving instances of the problem. An experimental study is done using both artificial instances generated according to the Impartial Culture (IC) and Mallows models and data from real-world electoral polls. This study assesses the ability of the genetic algorithm to identify feasible solutions and analyses the type of instances for which it is easier to construct an electoral system that leads to the election of the desired candidate.
